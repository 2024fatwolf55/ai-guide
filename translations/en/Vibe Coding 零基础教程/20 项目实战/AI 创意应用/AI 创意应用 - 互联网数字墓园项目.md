# AI Creative Application - Internet Digital Cemetery Project Case Study

This project is an interesting “internet digital cemetery” website created to commemorate once-glorious internet products that have already shut down, including Kaixin001, Qvod, TTPlayer, Tudou, and others.

This article does not cover the development process. I’m simply sharing a typical AI creative application case to help broaden your thinking.



---



Hello everyone, I’m Yupi. Let me share a pretty interesting project: I used AI to create an `internet digital cemetery` to memorialize those internet products that were once brilliant.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20250803162751255.png)

The idea came pretty suddenly. A few days ago, while casually browsing online, I came across Renren, only to find that it had officially shut down in December 2024. I instantly felt all kinds of emotions. Back when I was still in elementary school, I specifically registered for Renren just to play Happy Farm, setting alarms every day to steal vegetables. The kind of joy we had back then is probably something kids born in the 2000s and 2010s will never experience.

Then I started thinking: after so many years of internet development, how many websites and apps that once accompanied us have quietly disappeared? These memories of the digital era deserve a place to be preserved properly. So I came up with the idea of making a digital cemetery dedicated to recording internet products that have already shut down.

I used AI for the entire website—from design to collecting materials to development—and finished it within an hour. I chose a cemetery theme with black and gray tones and added some subtle animation effects to create a commemorative atmosphere.

Honestly, the final effect was even better than I expected, so I specifically deployed it online and even got a domain for it: https://gualeme.cn (“Is it dead?”). You can visit it too—maybe it’ll awaken your DNA~

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20250803163450884.png)

The website includes all kinds of domestic and international websites and apps that shut down between 2009 and 2024, sorted by year. Each product has a short introduction and an exact “date of death.”

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20250803194606031.png)

Let me mention a few classic Chinese products. They really are the youth memories of an entire generation.



## Revisiting Classic Products



### Renren

As the Chinese version of Facebook, how much youth of the ’80s and ’90s generations did it carry? Back then I played all kinds of mini-games on it endlessly. Socializing at that time really was simple and pure. Sadly, it ultimately said its formal goodbye to us on December 2, 2024.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20250803164326361.png)



### TTPlayer

This is the first music player I remember using, and I still remember the first song I listened to with it was “A Thousand Miles Away.” That clean interface, plus the synchronized lyrics feature—which felt like black technology at the time—accompanied so many people through their student days. After being acquired by Baidu in 2013, it was renamed Baidu Music and continued to exist in another form, but the pure TTPlayer era really did come to an end.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/f6ecbca783ff2cd-1.png)



### Tudou

Founded in 2005, Tudou launched with the slogan “Everyone is the director of life.” In the era before Douyin and Kuaishou, Tudou was one of the main platforms where we shared videos and showed creativity. It was also the first platform where I ever uploaded a video. I still remember uploading the little skits and game recordings I shot myself, hoping the views would break 10.

After 2012, Tudou merged with Youku, and in 2017 it was fully absorbed by Youku. Its decline from independent brand to complete disappearance dragged on for several years. Kids today probably haven’t even heard of it.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20250803164620995.png)



### Tencent Weibo

Launched in 2010, it grew quickly thanks to QQ’s huge user base, and by 2011 its registered user count had even surpassed Sina Weibo. But WeChat’s rise completely changed the rules of the game. Tencent realized WeChat fit its strategy much better than Weibo, so Tencent Weibo was strategically abandoned. It was basically in a “vegetative state” starting in 2014 and only officially announced the end of service in 2020.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20250803165127414.png)



### Qvod

Among all the products that shut down, Qvod is probably one of the most lamentable. In April 2014, Qvod’s servers were shut down, and related people were sentenced as well. As for why it shut down and why people got sentenced, I won’t say much more—those who know, know. Technically speaking, Qvod really was innovative. It was just used in the wrong place.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/2025020712054687015.png)



## Final Thoughts

There are many other sites too, like China Mobile’s Fetion, Xiami Music, Tianya Club, and more, which I won’t expand on here. Every disappeared product has its own story. Although they’ve declined, the marks they left on the history of internet development can’t be erased, and they also carry the youth memories of our generation.

Although this project is simple, it demonstrates an important AI coding use case: quickly turning creative ideas into reality. When you have a good idea, you no longer need to spend huge amounts of time learning complicated technology. You can just use AI tools to turn that idea into a real product in a short time.

I hope this case gives you some inspiration and helps you realize that AI coding isn’t only for serious commercial projects. It can also be used to build all kinds of fun ideas. Who knows—maybe the next breakout creative app will be yours?
