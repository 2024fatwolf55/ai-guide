# Yupi’s AI Workflow Sharing

Recently I watched Tim from Storm Crew share his team’s AI workflow. Their team mainly produces video content, and they use AI to assist with topic selection, concept clips, voice generation, and more. Their efficiency has improved dramatically.

As a programmer + knowledge creator + coding instructor + entrepreneur, the things I deal with every day are even more numerous and messy than video production: writing code, writing articles, shooting videos, running a company, building products, answering user questions, and so on...

So I probably rely on AI more heavily than most people.

Starting this year, I’ve personally been consuming around **1 billion** tokens every month, and I’ve already burned through a cumulative 4 billion tokens. That’s because I’ve turned almost all of my repetitive work into AI workflows.

What exactly is a workflow?

You can think of it as a fixed set of operating standards and processes, or simply as an Agent Skills package. AI can execute tasks according to that process, and I only need to give it a trigger signal.

Let me share some of my own AI workflows with you. Hopefully they’ll give you a few ideas.

## 1. Hot Topic Discovery and Content Ideation

I used AI coding to build a “whole-network hot topic monitoring tool” and packaged a “content topic discovery workflow.”

With the hot topic monitoring tool, I can enter the keywords I want to monitor, and AI will regularly crawl relevant information from across the internet and perform intelligent analysis.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260304102630302.png)

Whenever I need trending topics or inspiration, I can open the content topic discovery workflow in my AI coding tool and simply send AI the four characters “今日选题”. AI will automatically pull popular information from across the web and output a hot-topic report sorted by popularity, including information sources and suggested content angles.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503114738392.png)



## 2. Article and Image Creation

I’ve continuously polished and optimized a “content creation workflow” that supports both long-form articles and short image-and-text posts.

I only need to provide AI with the topic or a draft tutorial. It first researches related information across the web, then generates an outline for my confirmation. Once I confirm it, it writes the full content in my writing style. I then manually refine and adjust it, and finally let AI do one more pass to check for factual errors.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503115117329.png)

Inside this workflow, I wrote a complete style guide for AI, telling it what kinds of phrasing I like and what empty clichés I hate. The generated content has become increasingly close to how I would write it myself.

For knowledge-explanation content, I ask AI to split the article into sections and generate AI image prompts for each section, then throw those prompts into Nano Banana or GPT Image to create the visuals. I barely need to draw anything myself anymore. Article covers and tutorial posters are also generated by AI now. I just describe the style and elements I want.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503115326211.png)

But to be honest, the efficiency gains in article-and-image creation vary a lot depending on the type of content. For example, tutorial content requires a huge number of hands-on screenshots. I usually complete the actual operations myself first and then give the draft to AI for polishing, so this part still takes a lot of time. Writing hot-news content isn’t easy either. Most of the time is spent on manual verification, because AI is inherently stochastic and can hallucinate at any moment. When it comes to factual content, humans still have to be the final gatekeepers.

After finishing an article, I use the “all-platform article sync assistant” I built with AI coding to push the article into draft boxes on more than 10 platforms, including WeChat Official Accounts, Zhihu, and Bilibili, with one click. Then I manually adjust and publish it. In the past, syncing one article to all those platforms took nearly an hour of repetitive copy-pasting, formatting, and image uploading. Now I just click once and it’s done.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503115936341.png)



## 3. Programming Development

**This is not an exaggeration: 99% of my code now is written by AI.**

For example, I once needed an automation tool to handle daily operations for an e-commerce platform. Doing it manually used to take one or two hours every day, and it was all repetitive, meaningless work.

So I wrote a requirements document and handed it to AI, which helped me build a Chrome browser extension. Now the extension runs fully automatically in the background, regularly checks orders, and processes feedback on its own, with no manual management required.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503120446920.png)

If I had developed that purely by hand in the past, a complex browser extension spanning multiple websites would have taken at least a week. With AI coding, the whole thing took less than a day from start to finish.

Besides writing code, AI is also extremely useful for source code analysis. When I was learning the Claude Code source, I first had AI analyze the entire codebase, then used it as my Q&A assistant. Whenever I didn’t understand a design choice, I asked it directly. I even had AI generate a source-code analysis website, which was far more efficient than reading everything line by line myself.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503120818804.png)



## 4. Problem Solving

All kinds of problems in work and life can be solved with AI’s help.

For example, I once bound the wrong bank card to an account on a certain platform. In the past, I probably would have called customer service several times, gotten bounced around, and eventually given up. But this time I had AI negotiate with customer service for me, generating all kinds of scripts and appeal materials. After nearly a month, the issue was actually resolved. After all, AI doesn’t get annoyed or tired, and it generates formal wording quickly.

When filling out forms, I also just take a screenshot of the webpage, or copy the page structure to AI, and let it generate the fields I need to fill in. Then I simply copy and paste them. Feels great~

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503121244344.png)



## 5. Company Management

In the past, for finance-related company questions, I either asked the finance team or searched online myself. But the information online is all over the place, and it’s very easy to get misled or fooled.

And for things like contract clauses, there’s no way I’m going to pay a lawyer to review every single one.

Now I directly hand AI all of the company’s background information, financial data, payroll sheets, invoices, and so on, and let it suggest reasonable tax-planning ideas, analyze the company’s revenue situation, and review legal documents such as contracts.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503121721974.png)

For important information, I cross-check it with multiple AI models at the same time. I never trust the conclusion of just one.



## 6. Data Analysis

Although our team has its own data-monitoring dashboard, if I want to check a new business metric, such as comparing paid conversion rates between last week and this week, I still have to write SQL queries myself or ask an engineer to help. Sometimes it takes a long time just to get one number.

Now I’ve connected AI directly to the business database in read-only mode. I can tell it in natural language what data I want to see, and it automatically turns that into an SQL query and gives me an analysis report. I can get the result in the time it takes to say one sentence.

This is actually the classic NL2SQL pattern, translating natural language into SQL. There are quite a few open-source projects for this online, such as Chat2DB, which can save you the time of building the workflow yourself.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503122348759.png)



## 7. Video Creation

I haven’t built any fully automated video-generation workflow. That approach may save effort, but it lacks warmth and authenticity.

What I do more often is use AI to save time on repetitive work and improve efficiency in scenarios like these:

**1) Demo Website Generation**

For some tutorial videos, I used to find visuals for the video myself. Now I just let AI generate an interactive PPT-style demo website based on the written article, and I can explain it directly while presenting.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503123105211.png)

**2) B-roll Generation**

In the past, to find suitable B-roll, I had to browse stock footage sites one by one and keep copyright risks in mind. Now, with AI video-generation models like Seedance from Jimeng, I can input prompt text and generate creative videos directly, making the visuals richer.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503123619721.png)

**3) Subtitle Repair**

I often use Jianying to edit videos, and the subtitles it auto-generates always contain typos and awkward sentence breaks. I used to correct them one by one manually, which was both boring and time-consuming, especially for tutorial videos packed with English technical terms. Fixing those was even more painful.

Now I just throw the subtitle file to AI, let it automatically fix it based on the script I already wrote, and then import it back into Jianying. It feels amazing~

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503123858942.png)

**4) Metadata Generation**

Before publishing a video, I let AI generate the information needed for posting across platforms, such as titles, tags, descriptions, cover suggestions, and so on. Different platforms have different vibes, and it can output targeted versions for each one.

![](https://pic.yupi.icu/1/image-20260503124123757.png)

Although some of the generated content still feels a little stiff, it gives me plenty of inspiration. After a bit of manual polishing, it’s ready to publish~



## Final Thoughts

That’s my AI workflow sharing. As you can see, I’ve completely integrated AI into my work. Tokens are my productivity now.

Don’t think building AI workflows is a hassle. None of these workflows were built in just one or two days. Instead, I kept discovering problems in my work, trying to use AI to solve them, then letting AI solidify those solutions into reusable workflows, and finally improving them through continuous iteration until they became the AI super-tools that fit me best.

Any repetitive work you do can be improved through AI coding or workflow packaging. And while using AI, you need to keep giving it feedback, injecting your own thinking, habits of expression, and working style. That’s the only way AI will become more and more suited to you.

Get moving and build your own AI workflows!
